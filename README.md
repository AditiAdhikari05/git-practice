Article I find interesting related to software engineering is [The Impact of AI on Software Engineers](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-2026)

Why I like the Article?

What stands out most in the article is the three-way split into "Builders," "Shippers," and "Coasters." It's a more useful lens than the usual "AI makes everyone faster" narrative, because it captures how the same tools produce different outcomes depending on what an engineer already optimizes for. Builders end up spending more time reviewing AI-generated code than writing it themselves, which is a strange kind of productivity gain — you ship more, but the bottleneck just moves from typing to judgment.

The cost data is the other piece worth sitting with. Companies paying 5-10x what individual engineers pay out of pocket, combined with usage caps already hitting a third of respondents, suggests the current pricing is propped up by subsidized enterprise deals rather than sustainable unit economics. That gap between what a tool costs today and what it might cost once subsidies fade feels like the real story hiding underneath the productivity headlines.
